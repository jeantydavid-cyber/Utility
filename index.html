Small calculators that do the arithmetic properly

Three tools, each built around a number that most converters get wrong: the density of the ingredient, the efficiency of the appliance, the capacity of the egg yolk. No accounts, no tracking you didn't agree to, results as you type.

Cups to grams

Volume to weight, per ingredient. A cup of honey weighs four times a cup of cocoa — one conversion factor cannot serve both.

Heating cost

Gas, oil, pellets, firewood, electricity and heat pumps on one scale: euro per kWh of heat actually delivered into the room.

Mayonnaise helper

Quantities from the amount of oil you have, and a rescue guide for when the emulsion goes wrong — which it does, for everyone.

Why these three?

Each one exists because the obvious shortcut gives a wrong answer. Generic cup converters quietly use water's density for everything, which is why online-converted baking recipes fail. Heating fuels are priced in incompatible units — cubic metres, litres, kilograms, kilowatt-hours — so the invoice price can't be compared until it's converted to the cost of delivered heat, appliance efficiency included. And mayonnaise recipes state quantities the wrong way around: the yolk count should follow from the oil, not the reverse, because it's the yolk's coating capacity that sets the limit.

Every tool explains its own arithmetic below the calculator, including what it deliberately leaves out. If a tool's honest answer is "buy a €15 kitchen scale and stop converting", it says so.
